How fast should you respond to a new real estate lead?

The short answer is: as fast as you possibly can, in a way that sounds like you. Here is a first-message script and a 30-day follow-up plan.

By the N3X Realty team

The short answer: as fast as you can, and certainly the same day. When someone fills in a form or asks about a listing, they're paying attention right now. A few hours later they may have called another agent, or simply moved on with their evening.

Speed alone isn't the goal, though. A fast reply that sounds like a robot can do more harm than a slower one that sounds human. The aim is a quick, personal first touch, then steady follow-up for the weeks it usually takes someone to be ready.

Why the first reply matters so much

  • Attention fades quickly. A buyer browsing listings at 9 pm is often contacting several agents at once. The first useful reply sets the tone.
  • Most leads aren't ready today. A quick reply starts a conversation, and the conversation is what turns into a client months later.
  • It shows how you'll work. A prompt reply tells sellers you'll be just as responsive with their buyers.

What to say in your first message

Keep it short, specific and easy to answer. Text usually works better than email for a first touch, as long as the person gave you their number and agreed to be contacted.

Hi Kelly, this is Sam with Your Brand Realty. Thanks for asking about 123 Maple. It's still available. Are you hoping to move in the next few months, or just starting to look?

That message does three things: it says who you are, answers the question they asked, and ends with an easy question that starts a conversation.

Qualify gently

Over the next few messages, learn the basics without making it feel like a form:

  • Timing: when they'd like to move.
  • Financing: whether they've spoken with a lender.
  • Must-haves: area, bedrooms, budget range.
  • Selling: whether they need to sell a home first.

A simple 30-day follow-up plan

If they go quiet, don't give up after one message. A light, useful cadence keeps you in mind without being pushy:

  1. Day 0: first text within minutes, then a short email with your contact details.
  2. Day 1: a call. If there's no answer, a brief voicemail and a text saying you called.
  3. Day 3: send two or three listings that match what they asked about.
  4. Day 7: share something useful, like a quick note on how the local market is moving.
  5. Day 14: a check-in question: "Still looking in North Austin, or have plans changed?"
  6. Day 30: invite them to a monthly listings update or a market report, so you stay in touch long term.

Covering nights and weekends

Many inquiries arrive when you're showing homes, at dinner or asleep. You have three realistic options:

  • An auto-reply that acknowledges the message and says when you'll follow up.
  • A teammate or inside sales agent who covers set hours.
  • AI follow-up that replies in your voice, asks the first qualifying questions and hands the conversation to you once the person is engaged.

Whichever you choose, read the conversations regularly and step in personally once someone is serious.

Stay compliant

Text messaging is regulated. Get clear consent before you text, identify yourself, include a way to opt out (such as "Reply STOP to stop"), respect quiet hours, and follow federal rules like the TCPA plus any state rules. Your brokerage may have its own policy too.

The takeaway

Reply fast, sound human, ask one easy question, and follow up on a plan instead of when you happen to remember. That combination wins more conversations than any single script.
