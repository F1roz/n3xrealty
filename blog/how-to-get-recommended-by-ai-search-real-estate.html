How real estate agents can get recommended by ChatGPT and AI search

More buyers and sellers now ask AI assistants who to work with. Here is what influences those answers and what you can do about it.

By the N3X Realty team

More people now ask an AI assistant questions like "Who's a good realtor for first-time buyers in Plano?" instead of scrolling through search results. The assistant gives a short answer with a few names. Generative engine optimization, or GEO, is the work of making sure your name can be one of them.

No one can guarantee what an AI tool will say, and the tools change often. But they tend to draw on the same kinds of public information, and you control much of it.

What AI assistants look at

Assistants that search the web build answers from sources they can find and trust. For a local agent, that usually means:

  • Your own website, especially pages that clearly state who you are, where you work and who you help.
  • Your Google Business Profile and other directory listings.
  • Reviews on major platforms.
  • Mentions in local news, community sites and partner websites.
  • Helpful content that answers the exact questions people ask.

1. Make your website easy to understand

  • Say plainly on your home and about pages: your name, brokerage, the areas you serve and the clients you specialize in.
  • Write an FAQ in the words clients use: "How much do I need for a down payment in Texas?", "How long does it take to sell a house in Plano?"
  • Add structured data (schema markup) for a real estate agent, so machines can read your name, area and contact details reliably.

2. Publish local, specific content

Generic articles about "5 tips for buyers" compete with thousands of others. Content about your area doesn't:

  • Neighborhood guides covering schools, commute, parks and the kinds of homes available.
  • Monthly market notes for your area, written in plain language.
  • Guides for your specialty, such as relocation, investors, or first-time buyers in your state.

3. Keep your information consistent everywhere

Use the same name, phone number, website and service areas on your website, Google Business Profile, Zillow, Realtor.com, your brokerage page and social profiles. Conflicting details make it harder for any system to be confident they're all about you.

4. Earn real reviews and mentions

  • Ask satisfied clients for reviews on Google and the portals you use.
  • Contribute to local publications, community groups and partner blogs, such as lenders, builders or relocation companies.
  • Never fake reviews or endorsements. Besides being against platform rules, fake reviews are illegal under US consumer protection law.

5. Check how AI tools describe you

Every month or two, ask a few assistants the questions your clients would ask, such as "best realtor for first-time buyers in [your city]", and note who appears and which sources are cited. If the answer gets your details wrong, fix the source it seems to rely on.

GEO and SEO work together

Most of this is good local SEO done well: a clear website, consistent listings, genuine reviews and useful local content. The difference is the emphasis on answering real questions directly, because that's what AI assistants are trying to do.
